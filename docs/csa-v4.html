One coherent physical unit

Input and mirror devices use segmented shared diffusion, matched interleaving and dummies, with common guards and dense vias. The feedback MOM can be replaced for loop experiments; the complete extraction retains its actual routes and mutual coupling. The shared reference remains outside the per-channel unit. A single layout has zero hierarchical instances.

Strict comparison retains MOS-reduction and identical-parallel-MOM identity warnings; it does not waive property errors. Warm raw-amplifier gain is about 82 dB, but unity-buffer margin is inadequate; cold unity feedback is unstable. Those diagnostics do not replace the measured capacitive-feedback CSA loop. This is a CSA, not a qualified general-purpose unity buffer.

Bandwidth, loop margin and finite settling

The 24-point matrix spans 0.3/3/6 pF detector capacitance and 20/100 fF external output load. The nominal complete-unit PEX reaches 10.03–10.17 MHz warm and 10.75–11.09 MHz cold, with Cf-branch margin above 65°. Warm reserve is small. At 6 pF the bandwidth is about 4.6–5.1 MHz; compensation has a separate LIGHT mode for 0.3 pF.

All measured frequency points

Independent 0.25-fC calibration precedes the ±20/50-mV holdouts. Repeatable gain is removed, while short-time settling errors remain. PEX 1% settling takes roughly 113–141 ns. A 50-MHz sampling interval is only 20 ns; a fast edge can move charge between several samples even when its settled total is accurate.

Each line retains one of four arrival phases at 5, 10, 20 and 50 MHz. These are sharp-edge, noise-off settling stresses, not predictions for every physical detector waveform.

Noise and reset-free recovery

Frequency noise is integrated over 10 kHz–50 MHz. Two short native transient-noise seeds follow frequency optimization and retain the frozen noise-off calibration. The 25.2-µs transient records request those noise parameters but Spectre reports noisefmin ≈39.68 kHz and noisefmax =50 MHz; the original raw logs are audited separately. Below noisefmin the generated source PSD is held flat: this parameter is the lower end of colored-noise modeling, not a hard high-pass cutoff. At nominal 3 pF the full PEX records give about 0.152–0.162 fC warm and 0.091–0.097 fC cold. These finite records are not an ensemble PSD estimate or measured cryogenic noise qualification.

Declared negative-background-assisted entry has twelve matched training-disabled controls. Entry from the prebiased 0.85-V output requires about 1.25–1.30 fC of negative charge. Twelve overload/returned-charge trials recover and accept a following small event. The positive return current is an external diagnostic; supply-ramp startup and recovery using the actual replenisher remain to be qualified.

Sharing the reference without hiding heat

The original warm four-channel bias bus misses the predeclared 1% coupling screen. Its measured impedance motivates two shared 64-square MOM bypasses across the reference's VSG. They add about 1.36 pF of modeled bypass and no extra bias current. This is a small one/four-channel demonstration; ideal common buses do not substitute for a placement extraction.

Four-channel PEX pulse coupling is 0.687% warm / 0.492% cold. Total upstream group power divided by four is about 37.86 / 21.30 µW, including the one shared reference. Warm group bandwidth is 9.85 MHz. Extra exploratory power is allowed; the 24-µW analog reference allocation is a target, not a candidate cutoff.

Numerical controls and evidence

The short zero/1/10-aF numerical-capacitance controls have identical transistor/passive geometry. All three are warning-free and pass their declared edge, charge and source-work comparisons. Complete-channel upstream energy

The native audit counts complete EN pulses in the same whole-clock windows as the native source-energy integrals. Every supply, reference, clock, bank, driver and positive external-port contribution remains included; reservoir changes are never subtracted. Shared GEN/BANK totals are unallocated, so these are total fixture costs. No chip channel count is assumed.

\[E_{\rm upstream}=\sum_{k=1}^{7}E_{{\rm source},k}+E_{\rm positive\ ports},\qquad E_{\rm operation}=\frac{E_{\rm upstream}}{N_{\rm complete\ EN}},\qquad E_{\rm clock}=\frac{E_{\rm upstream}}{N_{\rm clock}}.\]

With zero operations, energy per operation is undefined; power and energy per clock remain nonzero. This energy per operation depends on activity and window duration, and each EN pulse need not represent identical calibrated charge across variants. The long calibration and short event therefore have different costs per operation even at similar average draw. Numerical-warning records retain their diagnostic status.
