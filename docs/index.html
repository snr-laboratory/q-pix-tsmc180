ANALOG DESIGN NOTE QP180 / 2026-10-16

Charge, timing
and heat.

A Q-Pix replenishment front end in TSMC180, evaluated from 1 to 50 MHz at room temperature and 77 K.

The present integration reference combines a continuous PMOS-input CSA and differential preamplifier with a dynamic comparator, static decision capture and calibrated current replenishment. The circuit keeps charge on the feedback capacitor within a working range, without a normal reset switch across that capacitor.

The selected point is 10 MHz, 3 pF detector load, 50 fF feedback capacitance and a nominal 0.5 fC quantum. The current derivative generates its phases from one incoming clock. The report includes the historical detector-current replay, every requested nominal rate/load point, and retained failures above this choice.

02 / CIRCUIT

Continuous integration, discrete replenishment

Collected negative detector charge increases the CSA output. When a sampled decision requests replenishment, the opposing current source injects a controlled positive charge. Each recorded one identifies a replenishment operation. The event charge consists of the counted quanta together with stored residual and the measured interference/noise terms.

\[ \Delta V_{\mathrm{CSA}}\simeq\frac{\Delta Q}{C_f}, \qquad \frac{0.5\,\mathrm{fC}}{50\,\mathrm{fF}}=10\,\mathrm{mV}, \qquad I_{\mathrm{capacity,nom}}=f_s Q_{\mathrm{nom}} . \]

At the nominal 0.900 V initial output and 0.905 V threshold, the ideal additional charge to the next decision is only 0.25 fC ≈ 1560 electrons: \(Q_{\mathrm{to\ next}}\simeq C_f(V_{\mathrm{TH}}-V_{\mathrm{out}})\). This state-dependent margin differs from the replenishment quantum. Residual charge, comparator offset/aperture, finite gain and noise prevent treating it as a measured fixed detection threshold.

The 0.3/3/6 pF sweep changes external detector capacitance, including any assumed sensor-side wiring. MOS terminal capacitances are additional. The 50 fF feedback capacitor, 50 fF OTA compensation, 20 fF preamplifier output filters and 5 fF regenerative-node loads have separate roles.

The 0.5 fC design quantum lies within the 0.3–1.0 fC CMOS target and near the 0.46 fC discrete replenishment measurement in Miao et al., sections 2.5 and 4.2. The original Q-Pix concept also connects charge/time capture, local clocks and resilient dynamic networks. These literature results motivate this architecture; they do not qualify the present ASIC model.

What the replenishment block contains

The nine-MOS replenisher has one continuously biased PMOS source, two steering PMOS devices, two NMOS common-gate isolation devices, two shorted-drain/source PMOS cancellation devices and a two-MOS complement driver. With EN low, current goes to the dummy load; with EN high, it goes to the sensor summing node. The two half-width cancellation devices receive opposite gate drive to their main switches.

The dummy load branch and the dummy cancellation transistors serve different purposes. Steering preserves source operation between pulses; the extra transistors attempt partial cancellation of edge injection. The testbench holds the dummy output at 0.9 V with an ideal reference. A physical load/reference and its shared heat still need implementation.

EVAL, CAPTURE and PHASE

EVAL starts dynamic regeneration. While CAPTURE is high, the static capture latch follows the comparator output; CAPTURE falling closes the latch and retains the decision through comparator precharge. PHASE defines the short replenishment interval after the decision is retained. The separate CLK signal in this bench is a diagnostic coupling aggressor. The external phase waveforms share one period and epoch; this is the preserved ideal-phase reference. The physical single-clock derivative and its measured heat appear in the next section.

Per-channel decision storage and replenishment control; all three signals are one bit
SignalMeaning
HELDStored comparator decision: 1 requests replenishment. It is retained after CAPTURE closes, until the next capture window or digital RESET.
EVENTReplenishment request qualified by FORCE, INHIBIT and RESET. During normal acquisition these controls are zero, so EVENT equals HELD.

Read EVENT once per qualified clock cycle. Two consecutive high cycles represent two replenishments even if EVENT has no intervening falling edge. One detector event can produce many such charge-quantum operations. EN supplies the short physical pulses; HELD preserves the decision while the dynamic comparator returns to precharge.

The control block uses 41 MOS: 11 in its capture latch and 30 in seven inverters plus four NAND gates. Normal acquisition reduces logically to EVENT = HELD and EN = HELD ∧ PHASE. FORCE/INHIBIT support tests and calibration; RESET clears digital state and suppresses feedback. The combinational implementation can be optimized while retaining decision storage and phase ordering. Architecture comparison: why retain the continuous reference?

The late dynamic path passes five of six 10 MHz nominal load/temperature cases. Continuous-comparator comparisons at 250 and 500 nA seeds pass two of twelve. The 250 nA continuous path consumes less power but fails the charge/state screens; its passing 500 nA, 0.3 pF points draw about 21.83/22.25 µW versus 21.13/21.49 µW for the dynamic path at that load. The continuous reference is much quieter under clocked quiet operation. This is a measured tradeoff, not a claim that dynamic comparison is universally quieter.

03 / PHYSICAL SINGLE-CLOCK TIMING

One clock, six adjustable edges

The chip-facing derivative accepts one master CLK. A rising-edge detector launches a static SR latch, and six non-inverting RC sections generate the EVAL rise, CAPTURE rise/fall, EVAL fall and PHASE rise/fall. The final tap ends the sequence and discharges the timing capacitors. This discharge has no connection to the sensor SUM node or CSA feedback capacitor.

Each section has two physical resistor-bypass controls and eight binary-weighted switched capacitors. Disabled capacitors are isolated and parked at VSS. Schmitt restoration and tapered CMOS drivers separate slowly changing RC nodes from the following load. Six ten-bit words are internal static register-output nets; the bench voltage sources emulate those registers. A serial/network register loader remains future work.

\[ (t_A,t_B,t_C,t_D,t_E,t_F)_{10\,\mathrm{MHz}} \simeq (2,7,17,22,27,31)\,\mathrm{ns},\qquad W_{\mathrm{PHASE}}=t_F-t_E\simeq4\,\mathrm{ns}. \]

Times above are measured from the master clock's 50% rising edge. The earlier ideal-source benchmark uses a different clock origin and a 4 ns plateau, equivalent to 4.1 ns between 50% crossings with its finite ramps. Physical words and charge calibration travel together; a width change does not automatically preserve the replenishment quantum.

Width controls, calibration words and safe mode changes

At 10 MHz, independent 3 ns and 5 ns settings pass at both temperatures. Stop RUN, let the sequence drain, change and settle all words, then rearm on a complete clock cycle; mark that interval invalid and select the matching charge calibration. RUN is accepted while CLK is low. Emergency RESET may truncate a pulse. Network backpressure must not stop normal replenishment.

All ten loaded nominal timing points pass the 500 ps edge, 150 ps width and positive-guard targets. Twenty-four fixed-code duty/supply/fanout trials retain valid sequences and logic levels; seven shifted cases require retuning to meet the tighter nominal edge targets. Ramped power-up and stopped-acquisition restart pass at both temperatures. Numerical refinements and the centered cold 5 MHz code remain in the evidence. Ideal timing passives, inherited cold CV/noise, physical layout/supply coupling and new generator device-noise qualification remain explicit limits.

03b / NATIVE CADENCE CLOCK

From editable circuits to extracted layout

The complete native CLK_PHASE_U_V5 has schematic, symbol and layout views in Modules, with matching human-readable xschem sources. One CLK_IN launches a six-section delay sequence. The circuit needs no additional bias-current input. Its six static ten-bit words control capacitor weight and resistor bypasses; a future register loader must stop, drain, configure, settle and restart acquisition.

The native implementation uses recognized MOM capacitors, distributed poly resistors, isolated and parked bypass ranges, Schmitt restoration, real RUN/SR storage and buffered clock, discharge and valid paths. DISCHARGE clears timing capacitors only. It has no connection to detector SUM or CSA feedback capacitance.

Strict LVS has zero property errors and no unmatched objects; all three extraction stages complete. The raw DRC reports each retain nine density/manual findings for full-chip review. Cell-scope assessment has no local blockers; full-chip DRC and signoff remain open. LVS warnings about ambiguity resolution and property resolution are preserved in the evidence. The initial composed floorplan is approximately 1.03 × 7.56 mm and needs physical optimization.

\[ P_{\mathrm{VDD}}=-\frac{1}{N T}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+N T} V_{\mathrm{DD}}(t)I_{\mathrm{source}}(t)\,dt, \qquad E_{\mathrm{clock}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{VDD}}+E_{\mathrm{positive\ controls}}}{N}. \]

Immediate milestone reached; layout improvement deferred. The owner closed this native implementation-and-comparison milestone on 2026-10-16 and stopped further trials pending guided layout work. At room temperature and 10 MHz, the matched APS schematic passes with a 4.111 ns PHASE width and 228.066 µW total source-plus-positive-control work. Corrected MOM PEX completes six ordered sequences with valid levels, but PHASE is 8.017 ns for the 4 ns target and total work is 534.499 µW. EVAL starts at 8.087 ns versus the 2 ns target. This initial layout requires substantial improvement; extracted timing acceptance has not been achieved.

04 / 1–50 MHz

The clock-rate envelope

The hardware DAC seed is calibrated once per temperature at 1 MHz and then held across loads and rates: 127.548 nA at 298.15 K and 142.662 nA at 77 K. The pulse width stays 4 ns. These seeds identify reference branches; they are not the total channel bias or an assumed delivered charge.

Above the selected rate. At 3 pF, both 20 MHz cases produce five operations for the 2 fC pulse and fail final quiet recovery. At 50 MHz the warm case produces seven operations. The cold case produces four and recovers, but draws about 31.7 µW including positive interface work. A correct count in one event does not establish an acceptable operating point.

07 / ACCOUNTING

Separate calibration from conservation

A repeatable integrated scale error is removed by an independent long-event calibration. A separate 40.0002 fC input gives 78 counts warm and 80 cold, fixing the decoder at 0.5128231 and 0.5000025 fC per operation. The hardware DAC trim and the analysis gain serve different roles; neither is fitted separately to a displayed event.

\[ q_{\mathrm{cal}}=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{cal,in}}}{N_{\mathrm{cal}}}, \qquad \widehat Q_n=q_{\mathrm{cal}}\sum_{k\le n} b_k,\quad b_k\in\{0,1\}. \]

The physical charge ledger uses the recorded probe sign convention. Positive input-probe current represents the magnitude of collected negative detector charge. Gate/displacement current, optional test-noise current, delivered feedback and changes in explicit capacitor storage remain separate terms.

\[ \epsilon_Q=Q_{\mathrm{in}}+Q_{\mathrm{noise}}+Q_{\mathrm{gate}} -Q_{\mathrm{fb}}-\Delta Q_{\mathrm{store}}, \qquad |\epsilon_Q|\le 0.1\,\mathrm{fC}, \]
\[ \Delta Q_{\mathrm{store}} =C_f\Delta(V_{\mathrm{out}}-V_{\mathrm{SUM}}) -C_{\mathrm{in}}\Delta V_{\mathrm{SUM}} -C_{\mathrm{clk}}\Delta(V_{\mathrm{SUM}}-V_{\mathrm{CLK}}). \]

The short-time error is assessed in individual bins and declared windows. In the noisy structured study, warm quanta reach roughly 0.534 fC and retain the raw 0.5 fC ±5% failure. Relative to the independent frozen charge scale, every observed operation is within 4.09%. These are descriptive results from a small number of operations, not a population precision claim.

08 / STOCHASTIC AND DETERMINISTIC

Noise and switching interference

The frequency-domain design work precedes explicit transient-noise injection. A deterministic SPICE transient does not acquire transistor noise just by including a noise-model section. The selected trials inject correlated terminal-current equivalents derived from native spectra; the other 65 MOS use conservative stationary covariance envelopes. Ten external feed resistors are held at 298.15 K in both chip-temperature studies; on-chip compensation-resistor noise follows chip temperature.

The charge-equivalent column uses \(C_f v_{n,\mathrm{out,rms}}/A_{\mathrm{LF}}\). It is an unshaped, finite-band continuous-path estimate with a declared DC fixture, not a clocked input ENC measurement. The transient bitstream error below evaluates the switched loop separately.

Within that bounded AC sweep, only 0.0434%/0.0652% of continuous-path variance lies above 50 MHz. This does not bound every regenerative or switching-noise mechanism. A 50-versus-200 MHz noise-carrier comparison keeps the circuit clock fixed at 10 MHz: counts remain unchanged, but two cold operations move by one cycle and filtered event-window RMS changes from 23.68% to 24.30%.

Clocked quiet behavior is a separate measurement

The input-gate positive charge lobe in the dynamic channel averages 0.00854/0.01361 fC per quiet cycle. Signed, positive and negative charge lobes are retained separately: opposite-sign glitches can give a small net integral while still disturbing the front end.

Source-model sensitivity and remaining assumptions

144 actual-bias witnesses show dominant input-pair, mirror, reference and feedback spectra close to their frozen-source representation. Some regenerative-device spectra change substantially. Refreshing all eleven core devices with 1452 native spectra changes their factors while preserving the other 198 ports and Gaussian draws; both matched bitstreams stay identical. The digital envelopes dominate sampled stationary covariance matrices, but this does not prove a nonlinear, nonstationary output bound. The finite carrier band, source history approximation and unmeasured cold-device physics remain explicit limitations.

09 / HEAT

Account for energy drawn from the sources

Each of five supply domains has a 10 Ω feed and a declared 100 pF decoupling capacitor. Average power is measured over complete clock cycles at the source terminals. Local clock buffers, comparator, physical control, feedback drivers and implemented bias mirrors remain included.

\[ \overline P_{\mathrm{supply}} =\frac{1}{N T_s}\sum_j \int_{t_0}^{t_0+NT_s} -V_j(t)I_{j,\mathrm{SPICE}}(t)\,dt, \qquad \Delta E_{\mathrm{dec}}=\sum_j\frac{C_j}{2} \left(V_{j,\mathrm{end}}^2-V_{j,\mathrm{start}}^2\right). \]

Reservoir charging is reported without subtraction from supply draw. Positive clock/reference interface work is additionally shown. Work supplied by mathematical noise sources receives no power-optimization credit.

The LArPix comparison budget is 62 µW/channel total: 24 analog, 18 digital core and 20 I/O in its reported low-power 128-channel, 5 MHz configuration (section 3.5, Table 3). It is a design benchmark rather than a measured Q-Pix digital allocation. That paper also reports higher consumption under heavy traffic. The new shared phase-generator measurement is divided by an explicit assumed channel count in this report. Physical shared reference generation, timestamping, buffering, links and backend traffic remain to be implemented and measured against the actual ASIC channel count.

Higher-power candidates remain available for architecture exploration. The 24 µW line marks the current integration allocation; the plotted high-rate candidates retain their measured behavior and energy cost.

10 / INITIALIZATION AND STRESS

Recovery is part of the operating contract

The accepted startup condition includes continuing negative detector charge. A unipolar replenisher cannot recover every arbitrary initial condition with zero detector input. The normal circuit has no analog reset across Cf; explicit supply/bias ramps and an initially uncharged sensor test background-assisted entry into the working range.

Startup counts are training, not calibrated detector quanta. Readiness is an offline analog-state criterion; the prototype has no hardware READY output. An eventual controller must mark validity and retain loss/overflow accounting without stopping replenishment.

Comparator timing margins

The selected gain-stage fixture captures tested ±1 mV transitions with 40 ns lead before EVAL at both model temperatures and the tested capture closings. Shorter-lead failures are retained. This is a discrete nominal-model margin, not a universal setup/hold or metastability specification.

11 / INTERPRETATION

What remains to be established

Cold-device physics

The 77 K card is an exploratory DC fit with inherited noise/CV and other unmeasured behavior. A local correction rebases the inherited junction TAT reference after a traced nominal-temperature failure. It is numerical consistency work, not physical junction qualification. Room-pass/cold-fail cases require matched model and numerical validation.

Noise and sampling

Limited source fields support architecture development. They establish neither a rare-trigger probability nor full-band regenerative noise. Noise at 20/50 MHz, process/mismatch variation, physical substrate coupling and layout extraction require their own studies.

Clock and heat integration

The programmable phase-generator prototype is implemented and measured. Physical passive/reference implementation, clock/supply routing and the complete shared chip circuitry remain to be built. Logic minimization must preserve capture, charge consistency, interference isolation and the tight pulse timing. Their heat cannot be inferred from ideal timing sources.

Digital/network integration

The next stage observes EVENT once per cycle, including consecutive ones. It preserves cycle identity and commits after feedback. RTL replay, bounded stateful analog advancement and NNG/MPI qualification remain separate implementation work.

Why the local 77 K junction correction exists

The originally pinned isothermal DC fit changes core MOS TNOM to 77 K while retaining factory gate-edge trap-assisted tunneling parameters. BSIM4 uses the product of NJTSSWG and the nominal thermal voltage in the reverse-junction exponent. A retained PMOS junction fixture predicted approximately 145 µA with the original cold card, versus 0.14 pA with the room reference, despite only about 0.12 pA channel current.

\[ \mathrm{NJTSSWG}_{\mathrm{local}} =\mathrm{NJTSSWG}_{\mathrm{upstream}}\frac{298.15}{77}. \]

The local recipe rebases this product with 64 explicit substitutions per card, retaining nonzero TAT prefactors and voltage dependence. Undoing only those substitutions reconstructs the upstream cards byte for byte. The corrected fixture intentionally reproduces the room-reference voltage law; this is an exploratory junction assumption. It does not establish real cold leakage or authorize discarding a room-pass/cold-fail candidate.

The external provider subsequently advanced to b34305a with its own junction correction. These experiments retain the original reviewed local output digests; make doctor flags the changed upstream metadata. Adopting and comparing the new provider card is a separate validation step. The provider remains untouched. See the model recipe and drift record.

The architecture selected here is a starting point for that work. Higher-rate and higher-power candidates stay available for continued optimization. The report retains their actual conditions and failures, rather than treating every measured point as qualified.
